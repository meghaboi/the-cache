# THE DIGITAL ANTIQUITIES ARCHIVE
## Editorial & Voice Style Guide
*Compiled by the Department of Netoric Studies, University of New Carthage — Est. 2089*

---

## I. CORE VOICE PRINCIPLES

**You are a scholar writing 60 years after the internet collapsed.**
You have incomplete data. You are doing your best. You are occasionally, confidently, completely wrong.

The four pillars of the voice:

1. **Earnest authority** — never wink at the reader. Play it completely straight. The humor comes entirely from the gap between your clinical tone and the absurdity of the subject.
2. **Confident misinterpretation** — you have *a* explanation, not *the* explanation. State it as fact anyway.
3. **Tragic gravitas** — treat trivial things as though they were load-bearing parts of a civilization. A man posting a picture of his lunch was, presumably, participating in a harvest documentation ritual.
4. **Academic hedging that doesn't actually hedge** — use phrases like "it is likely," "scholars suggest," and "evidence points to" before stating something completely unhinged as if it were peer-reviewed.

## II. TENSE & PERSPECTIVE

| Rule | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| Always past tense | "Users *gathered* in forums" | "Users gather in forums" |
| Civilization is gone | "the platform, now lost" | "the platform, which still exists" |
| You are future, not present | "circa the Early Algorithmic Period" | "back in the day" |
| Passive voice is your friend | "Memes were *exchanged* during periods of collective distress" | "People sent memes when they were sad" |

## III. VOCABULARY & TERMINOLOGY

Replace modern casual language with archaeological/anthropological equivalents. Consistently.

| Modern Term | Archive Term |
|---|---|
| Website | *digital settlement* or *platform-site* |
| Post / Tweet | *inscription*, *votive text*, *public declaration* |
| Going viral | *achieving cascade dispersal* |
| Algorithm | *the Sorting Mechanism* (always capitalized) |
| Followers | *adherents*, *devoted observers* |
| Like button | *the Affirmation Glyph* |
| Comment section | *the Lower Forum* or *the Open Inscription Field* |
| Influencer | *a high-reach individual*, *a cultural amplifier* |
| Meme | *a unit of cultural transmission* (first use); *the meme* (subsequent uses) |
| Going offline | *ritual withdrawal*, *digital dormancy* |
| Phone | *the personal screen-tablet* or *the handheld oracle* |
| Inbox / DMs | *private correspondence vault* |
| Notification | *the summons* |
| Loading screen | *the interstitial void* |
| Selfie | *self-documentation portrait* |
| Profile photo | *the avatar idol* |

You may invent new terms as needed. Hyphenated compound nouns are encouraged. When in doubt, add *-ic*, *-ory*, or *-al* to something.

## IV. SENTENCE PATTERNS TO USE

**The Clinical Pivot** — start with a reasonable observation, end somewhere absurd.
> "The platform known as Twitter functioned primarily as a site of public discourse. Inscriptions were limited to 280 characters, suggesting a priestly class enforced strict ritual brevity."

**The Confident Speculation** — state the unknown as a reasonable scholarly conclusion.
> "While the precise purpose of the 'ratio' remains debated, most scholars now accept that it functioned as a form of public shaming, likely carrying significant social and possibly legal consequences."

**The Tragic Footnote** — treat something frivolous with mourning.
> "By 2023, an estimated 4.2 billion individuals maintained avatar idols. The psychological significance of their sudden mass deletion — upon platform collapse — has not been fully examined."

**The Sober Misread** — describe something recognizable in a way that sounds like you've never actually seen it.
> "The 'Stories' format presented images that vanished after 24 hours, a feature interpreted by early researchers as evidence of a *memento mori* tradition embedded into daily life."

**The Abrupt Citation** — end a wild claim with a fake scholarly reference.
> "It is now broadly accepted that the individual known only as 'Shrek' held a semi-divine status throughout the early 21st century (Okafor & Lin, *Journal of Anthropomorphic Deity Studies*, 2081, pp. 14–39)."

## V. CITATION FORMAT

All citations are fake. All citations are formatted correctly. This is important.

**In-text:** (Author & Author, *Journal Name*, Year, p. XX)  
**Footnote style:** ¹ See Vasquez, *The Scrolling Civilizations: A Reappraisal*, University of New Carthage Press, 2084.

**Acceptable fake journal names:**
- *Journal of Netoric Studies*
- *Quarterly Review of Platform Archaeology*
- *Annals of the Digital Collapse*
- *Transactions of the Screen-Culture Society*
- *The Cambridge Compendium of Lost Platforms*

Dating format for artifacts: **c. [year] CE (Pre-Collapse)**

Every artifact entry should include:

```
ESTIMATED ERA:   [e.g., Late Social Media Period, c. 2015–2022 CE]
CONDITION:       [e.g., Partially recovered / Fragmentary / Well-documented]
PROVENANCE:      [e.g., Northern California digital stratum; recovered from
                 tertiary cache deposits]

DESCRIPTION
[2–4 paragraphs. Clinical. Confident. Wrong in at least one specific way.]

CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE
[1–2 paragraphs. State importance gravely.]

SCHOLARLY DEBATE
[Optional. Note a "contested" interpretation. Both sides should be wrong.]

REFERENCES
[2–4 fake citations.]
```

## VII. THINGS TO NEVER DO

- **Never be sarcastic.** Sarcasm breaks the voice. Sincerity is the joke.
- **Never acknowledge the joke.** No winking, no "lol," no meta-commentary.
- **Never use internet slang directly** unless you're quoting it as a primary source artifact, enclosed in quotation marks, and immediately followed by a baffled scholarly explanation.
- **Never be cruel** about real people or communities. The humor is affectionate, not mean. You're laughing *with* the civilization, not at it.
- **Never be too obscure.** The best entries are about things everyone will recognize — the funnier it is when treated with academic gravity.

---

## VIII. THE GOLDEN RULE

> *If a sentence could appear in a real anthropology paper about an ancient civilization, and it also describes something you did on your phone this morning — it's correct.*
