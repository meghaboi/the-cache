# THE CACHE
## Project Vision & Creative Direction
*Internal Document — Department of Netoric Studies, University of New Carthage*
*Version 1.0 — Pre-Launch Reference*

---

## WHAT IS THE CACHE

The Cache is a satirical future-archaeology website that documents early 21st century internet culture as if it were an ancient, collapsed civilization. It presents itself as a real institutional archive — maintained by fictional scholars in the year 2089 — cataloguing "recovered digital artefacts" from the Pre-Collapse Era.

The joke is never stated. The joke is everything.

The site operates on a single creative principle: **complete sincerity**. There is no winking, no irony signposted, no breaking of the fourth wall. The Cache behaves exactly like a real museum archive website would — and the humor emerges entirely from what it is cataloguing.

---

## THE WORLD IT IMPLIES

The Cache exists within a lightly-sketched fictional future:

- The internet as we know it **collapsed** at some point between 2040–2060 CE. The exact cause is not stated. Scholars debate it. (The debate is part of the content.)
- Most data was **lost or corrupted**. What survives are fragments — screenshots, cached text, partial video transcripts — recovered from "digital strata."
- A small academic field called **Netoric Studies** has emerged to make sense of what was found.
- The University of New Carthage (location unspecified) is the leading institution in this field.
- The Cache is their **public-facing catalogue** — the museum, not the lab.

None of this is explained directly on the site. It is implied through the writing, the metadata, the fake citations, and the institutional framing. Visitors figure it out within seconds. That figuring-out is part of the experience.

---

## WHO THIS IS FOR

**Primary audience:** People who grew up on the internet — millennials, Gen Z — who will immediately recognize every artifact being catalogued and find it funnier for it.

**Secondary audience:** Anyone who appreciates dry wit, institutional satire, and the kind of humor that commits fully to the bit.

**The reaction we're optimizing for:** Someone reads the entry on Doomscrolling, laughs, then immediately sends it to three people.

This is a **shareable project**. Every artifact page should be independently shareable and land without context. The site is the joke but each entry is also its own joke.

## CONTENT PHILOSOPHY

### Affectionate, Not Mean
The Cache is not making fun of internet culture. It is celebrating it — by treating it with the same gravity we give to genuinely ancient civilizations. The tone is warm. The civilization is gone. We miss it, in the way you miss something ridiculous that you loved.

### Confidently Wrong
The scholars of 2089 are doing their best with incomplete data. Their interpretations are logical but wrong. This is the engine of most of the humor — a reasonable extrapolation that misses the obvious explanation entirely.

### Escalating Commitment
The more obscure or absurd the artifact, the more scholarly apparatus should surround it. An entry on "Skibidi" should have three fake citations and a note about contested etymology.

### Depth Rewards Re-Reading
Surface visitors get the joke immediately. Close readers find more — fake footnotes, corrupted data, contradictions between entries, a slowly-building fictional world with internal consistency.

## COLLECTIONS OVERVIEW

The site is organized into six Collections, each with its own curatorial identity:

| Collection | What It Covers | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| **The Brainrot Collection** | Gen Z absurdist language & content | Baffled but earnest |
| **Rituals & Practices** | Documented user behaviours | Clinical, anthropological |
| **Objects & Relics** | Physical/digital objects of significance | Museum-label formal |
| **Deities & Figures** | Mythologized internet figures | Reverent, theological |
| **Languages & Scripts** | Linguistic artefacts, slang, emoji | Linguistic-academic |
| **Events & Epochs** | Major civilizational moments | Historiographical, grave |

Each collection has:
- A curatorial introduction written in voice
- A browsable index of artifact entries
- 3–10 entries at launch, expandable over time

## SITE SECTIONS

### Homepage
The front door of the archive. Institutional, calm, slightly imposing. Establishes the fictional world immediately without explaining it. Features a rotating "Currently Exhibiting" showcase and a short "About the Archive" statement.

### Catalogue Index
The full browsable catalogue. Filterable by Collection, Era, and Condition. Designed like a real museum database — utilitarian, dense with metadata.

### Individual Artifact Pages
The core content unit. Each page is a complete scholarly entry: description, cultural significance, scholarly debate, fake references. These are the shareable units.

### About the Archive
A straight-faced institutional "About" page for the University of New Carthage's Department of Netoric Studies. Written entirely in voice. Includes a fake department history, a note on methodology, and a list of "senior scholars" (fictional names, brief bios).

### The Scholars (Optional)
Fake academic bios for the team of fictional researchers. Each scholar has a specialty ("Prof. Amara Osei — Early Meme Dispersal, 2012–2018") and a photo (abstract or illustrated, never real people).

### Field Notes (Blog, Optional)
Occasional "dispatches" from ongoing excavations. Shorter, more informal entries. New discoveries. Corrections to previous entries. Ongoing scholarly disputes. Functions as a blog but never called a blog.

## LONG-TERM VISION

The Cache is designed to **grow indefinitely**. Internet culture produces new artifacts constantly. The backlog of existing artifacts is essentially unlimited. The site can expand without ever running out of material.

Potential future expansions:
- **User submissions** — "Submit an Artefact for Review" (moderated, written up in voice by the team)
- **The Disputed Archives** — entries where scholarly consensus has not been reached, written as ongoing debates
- **Physical exhibition** — printed catalogue zine, museum-style poster series
- **Audio guide** — podcast or audio entries read in character, dry documentary voice
- **Translations** — the archive "translated" for international audiences, with region-specific collections

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

- Individual artifact pages go viral on their own
- People share specific entries the way they share good tweets
- Someone somewhere debates whether it's a real archive before clicking through
- The project gets written up as "a love letter to internet culture"
- It becomes a reference — people say "there's a Cache entry for that"

---

## WHAT THE CACHE IS NOT

- It is not a nostalgia blog
- It is not a "cringe compilation"
- It is not mean-spirited
- It is not explained
- It is not finished
